For the Fluid Mechanics Dissection project, my groupmates and I were assigned a speaker. I contributed both analytically and experimentally to the team’s final project. I was responsible for developing approximately half of the presentation content that served as the script for the video. My primary contributions included the streamline and flow-field analysis, where I explained why classical incompressible-flow streamlines are not applicable to acoustic wave generation and instead modeled the speaker diaphragm as an unsteady, oscillating boundary producing compressible pressure waves governed by the continuity and Navier–Stokes equations. I also developed the “What If” parameter slides, analyzing how variations in cone area, oscillation frequency, and voice-coil gap affect volumetric flow rate, pressure amplitude, acoustic efficiency, and loudness and pitch. In addition, I created the governing-parameter analysis linking frequency, wavelength, amplitude, and cone geometry to the resulting fluid dynamic response. Alongside the analytical work, I actively participated in the physical dissection of the speaker and documented each step in the dissection worksheet, ensuring that the theoretical analysis was grounded in the actual device geometry and construction.
